\section{One sampled matching and one minimum-cost flow}
\label{simp:graph}

This section isolates the entire combinatorial step in one parameterized
rounding theorem.  It uses one auxiliary primal--dual pair.  No comparator
allocation is an algorithmic input, and no list of repaired scores is
computed.  The only local valuation facts used below are the score and
minorant certificates of Sections~\ref{simp:score-primitives} and
\ref{simp:minorants}, and the pointwise inequality of
Section~\ref{simp:completion-section}.

\subsection{The precise local interface}

Fix a feasible fractional allocation $x$ of the residual items.
Write $Q(S)$ for the maximum utility product obtained by giving each row
one reserved item after disjoint residual bundles $S_i$ have been chosen.
An ordinary label $e=(i,j)$ has a logarithmic score $\ell_e$ and an
outcome payoff $q_e(S_i)$; row $i$ also has a private score $\ell_{i0}$.
A new label has score $n_{ij}$ and payoff $q^{\mathrm N}_{ij}(S_i)$.
All scores in this section are logarithmic.

We use the following two explicit forms of local certificate.

\begin{enumerate}
\item A \emph{single-program certificate} supplies a normalized monotone
submodular function $g$ and its independent mean $G(x_i)$.  For every
fractional row with at most one fractional coordinate and $G(y)\ge G(x_i)$,
the resulting two-outcome law satisfies $\mathbb E q(S_i)\ge\ell$.
Constants are permitted.  Ordinary and private labels have this form.
The underlying raw valuation changes by at most $\alpha_i$ in this law.
\item An \emph{additive minorant certificate} supplies a constant $c_e$
and a normalized monotone submodular function $g_e$ such that
\[
 q_e(S_i)\ge c_e+g_e(S_i),\qquad
 c_e+G_e(x_i)\ge\ell_e.
\]
These certificates can be added with nonnegative weights: the stored
function is their weighted sum, and no individual mean is preserved.
Every new candidate and every supported ordinary label on a designated
compatible row has such a certificate.
\end{enumerate}

The pointwise completion contract is
\begin{equation}\label{simp:pointwise-contract}
 \log Q(S)\ge\sum_i d_iq_{i0}(S_i)
       +\sum_{e\in\mathrm O}a_eq_e(S_i)
       +\sum_{e\in\mathrm N}z_eq_e^{\mathrm N}(S_i)
\end{equation}
whenever each row has total mass one and the ordinary and new masses
use column capacity $a_e+2z_e$.  It holds on every common disjoint
outcome, with no independence assumption between rows.

\begin{lemma}[A common one-function rounding engine]\label{simp:forest}
Suppose each row has selected one single-program certificate or one
weighted sum of additive minorants.  There is a feasible joint rounding
of the residual items whose expected total payoff is at least the sum
of the selected scores, up to any prescribed positive error.  The same
engine is used for both types of row.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $d$ be the number of residual items and $N$ the number of initially
positive row--item coordinates.
First consider exact multilinear derivatives.  The multilinear
extension $G_i$ of a selected submodular function has nonnegative first
derivatives and nonpositive mixed second derivatives.  Consequently
$G_i$ is convex when two coordinates move in opposite directions.

On a cycle of the row--item support graph, choose opposite changes
at each item, so that its total load is fixed.  Choose derivative
ratios to make the directional derivative zero on all but one row;
reverse the common sign if necessary to make the last derivative
nonnegative.  Convexity shows that moving to the first boundary
decreases none of the $G_i$.  At least one support edge disappears.
Zero derivatives are handled by an arbitrarily small positive
regularization, or by the finite implementation below.  At most the
initial number of support edges is removed before the graph is a forest.

For each row, define its own marginal-preserving random program on this
forest point.  Pair two fractional coordinates $r,s$ and move in
direction $a_s e_r-a_r e_s$, where $a_r,a_s$ are the two derivatives.
Choose the two maximal feasible endpoints with their barycentric
probabilities.  The derivative at the start is zero, so convexity
preserves $G_i$ at either endpoint.  If both derivatives are zero,
round one coordinate by its marginal.  Every coordinate is a
martingale.  After at most $d-1$ pair steps only one fractional item
remains; round that last coin by its marginal.  Conditional on the
preceding history, this is a genuine two-outcome law with raw width
at most $\alpha_i$ and with the required stored mean.  A single-program
certificate applies to that law.  For a minorant row, preservation
of the mean of its sum suffices directly.

These row programs are coupled, rather than run independently.
Let $K(y,y')$ be a transition of a row program, including any estimator
states.  To condition its final parent-item bit $j$ to be $1$ or $0$,
use the direct kernels
\[
 K_j^+(y,y')=K(y,y')\frac{y'_j}{y_j},\qquad
 K_j^-(y,y')=K(y,y')\frac{1-y'_j}{1-y_j}.
\]
Coordinate martingality makes each requested kernel sum to one;
zero-probability conditions are never requested.  Root a support tree
at an item.  That item chooses row $i$ with its forest marginal $y_{ij}$,
or chooses none.  Each row runs its program conditional on its parent
item's bit.  At a child item $j$, if its parent row $i$ took it, all
child rows exclude it.  Otherwise choose child row $k$ with probability
$y_{kj}/(1-y_{ij})$, or choose none.  The capacity inequality makes
these probabilities legal.  Inductively, every row sees its prescribed
parent-bit marginal, so mixing its two conditional programs reconstructs
its \emph{entire} unconditional row law.  Every item is allocated at
most once.  Apply the row certificates and then
\eqref{simp:pointwise-contract} to this common outcome.

Here are the finite details needed by this primitive.  Normalize each
stored function to range at most one.  With derivative errors at most
$a$, clip negative estimates to zero and threshold zeros at $a$.
In a cycle move, all but one empirical directional derivatives vanish;
the true row mean loses at most $4a$ before the next boundary.
In a pair move the exact change, for signed step $v$, is
\[
 v(a_r\widehat a_s-a_s\widehat a_r)
   -v^2\widehat a_r\widehat a_s\partial_{rs}G_i.
\]
The quadratic term is nonnegative, and the linear error costs at most
$2a$ at either endpoint.  There are at most $N$ cycle moves and $d-1$
pair moves per row.  Downward dyadic resets add at most their total
coordinate change to the mean loss and never violate capacity.
Thus a score modulus $C$ is accommodated, for example, by
$a\le\epsilon/[100C(N+d+1)]$ and a total reset loss at most $a$.
The protected score primitives supply a finite computable modulus $C$.

Each fixed estimator outcome is followed by a barycentric step.
It therefore remains unweighted in the displayed conditional kernels.
Estimation failures are charged under the unconditional row programs,
whose complete laws the coupling preserves; no error is divided by a
rare conditioning probability.  Hoeffding sampling supplies each
range-one estimate using $\lceil2a^{-2}\log(2/p)\rceil$ samples.
All transition probabilities are rational after the indicated
approximations.  Approximate a categorical kernel by rounding all but
one of its positive probabilities down to a common dyadic denominator
and placing the remainder on the retained positive entry.  This creates
no forbidden transition.  Choosing the sum of the visited transition
errors below a prescribed $p$ bounds the total variation error by $p$.
Known reservation and full-set bounds pay the failure and coupling
errors.  Rational derivative ratios have polynomial bit length: in
pair rounding, charge additions of bit lengths to the elimination
tree of the disappearing coordinates; downward resets do the same
for the forest moves.  This gives polynomial work in the precision
and input/answer bit lengths, while keeping every history feasible.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The auxiliary problem and the generic repair contract}

Solve the following single linear program:
\begin{equation}\label{simp:hybrid-lp}
 \begin{split}
 \max\quad&\sum_i d_i\ell_{i0}+\sum_e a_e\ell_e+\sum_e z_en_e,\\
 &d_i+\sum_{e\ni i}a_e+\sum_{e\ni i}z_e=1,\qquad
   \sum_{e\ni j}a_e+2\sum_{e\ni j}z_e\le1,
 \qquad a,z,d\ge0.
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
Fix any complementary optimal primal--dual pair.  Its row values $v_i$
and nonnegative prices $\pi_j$ satisfy
\[
 v_i\ge\ell_{i0},\qquad v_i+\pi_j\ge\ell_{ij},\qquad
 v_i+2\pi_j\ge n_{ij},\qquad J=\sum_i v_i+\sum_j\pi_j.
\]
Write $\sigma_i=v_i-\ell_{i0}\ge0$.  A supported label has positive
primal mass and hence a tight dual constraint; $d_i\sigma_i=0$.

Fix a row class $A$, with complement $B$, and constants
\[
 r\ge2\ \text{integer},\qquad L>0,\quad K>L,\quad T>0,
 \quad P>0,\quad\frac12\le\theta\le1,\quad\kappa>0.
\]
The following is the complete parameter contract:
\begin{equation}\label{simp:graph-parameters}
 \boxed{\quad
 P\ge L+(1-\theta)T,\qquad
 \frac P{r-1}\le\frac K2,\qquad
 \frac{\max\{P,\theta T\}}{r-1}
                 \le\kappa(1-e^{-T}).\quad}
\end{equation}
The data must have these three properties.

\begin{enumerate}
\item Every row with supported new mass lies in $A$, and $d_i=0$ on
$A$.  Every supported ordinary label on $A$ has an additive minorant.
The private and ordinary labels on $B$ have single-program certificates.
\item Every auxiliary new label has a specified ordinary fallback in
the same auxiliary menu with score at least $n_{ij}-L$.
It follows immediately that every supported new head has
\begin{equation}\label{simp:new-price}
                         0\le\pi_j\le L.
\end{equation}
Indeed $v_i+2\pi_j=n_{ij}\le v_i+\pi_j+L$.
\item On each row of $A$, candidate scores $n_{ij}$ may also be provided
for labels omitted from the auxiliary LP.  Define
\[
 c_{ij}=(v_i+2\pi_j-n_{ij})^+,\qquad
 b_{ij}=1-e^{-c_{ij}},
\]
An unavailable zero-valued candidate has logarithmic score
$n_{ij}=-\infty$, and we set $c_{ij}=+\infty$, $b_{ij}=1$.
Every head with $c_{ij}\le T$ must have a new additive-minorant
certificate and an ordinary fallback of score at least $n_{ij}-L$.
There is no assumed dual inequality for an omitted candidate.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{theorem}[One-flow repair]\label{simp:repair-theorem}
Under this contract, one can sample an ordinary matching, solve one
integral minimum-cost flow, and apply Lemma~\ref{simp:forest} to obtain
a feasible output with
\begin{equation}\label{simp:repair-ledger}
 \begin{split}
 \mathbb E\log Q\ge J-\mathcal L-\epsilon,\qquad
 \mathcal L={}&\frac12\sum_j\min(\pi_j,K)
   +\frac1{r-1}\sum_{i\in B}\min(\sigma_i,P)\\
 &+\frac P{r-1}\sum_{i\in A}(r-h_i)^+
   +\kappa\sum_{i\in A}\sum_{\substack{j\in H_i\\\pi_j\le K}}
                      \min\{b_{ij},1-e^{-T}\}.
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
Here $H_i$ are \emph{any} pairwise disjoint comparator sets of reserved
heads, and $h_i=|H_i|$.  They are used only to bound the algorithm.
The expectation includes the matching sample and the residual rounding.
There is no requirement to realize an arbitrary mixture of ordinary
programs: every final $B$ row uses one program, and a two-label $A$ row
contains at most one ordinary label.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{A weighted matching lemma with mandatory columns}

We prove the only network inequality needed for the theorem.
Let $A',B'$ be rows, with an initial zero-cost ordinary matching $M_0$
covering all of $A'$ and every $B'$ row with $\sigma_i>0$.
Some columns of $M_0$ are mandatory.  Rows in $A'$ have supply two and
a capacity-one dummy arc of cost $P$; rows in $B'$ have supply one
and a private arc of cost $\sigma_i$.  Physical columns have capacity
one.  Physical costs $w_{ij}$ are nonnegative on $A'$ and zero on $B'$.
For disjoint sets $H'_i$ of available heads on $A'$, the optimum is at most
\begin{equation}\label{simp:mandatory-bound}
 \frac{1}{r-1}\left[
 \sum_{i\in B'}\min(\sigma_i,P)
 +P\sum_{i\in A'}(r-|H'_i|)^+
 +\sum_{i\in A'}\sum_{j\in H'_i}\min(w_{ij},P)\right].
\end{equation}
This is a bipartite min-cost flow with integral supplies and bounds,
so an integral optimum exists and is polynomially computable.

For completeness, its dual, after eliminating the dummy variables, is
\[
 D=\sum_{i\in A'}[\lambda_i+\min(\lambda_i,P)]
       +\sum_{i\in B'}\lambda_i-\sum_j\xi_j,
\]
subject to $\lambda_i-\xi_j\le w_{ij}$ on $A'$,
$\lambda_i\le\xi_j$ on $B'$, and $\lambda_i\le\sigma_i$ on $B'$.
An optional column has $\xi_j\ge0$; a mandatory column initially
allows either sign.  Row variables are initially unrestricted.

Replace negative variables by zero.  Feasibility survives because
all costs are nonnegative.  Every negative column is mandatory and
has a distinct initial owner with $\lambda_i\le\xi_j<0$.
The gain from raising this row variable is at least $-\lambda_i$,
which pays the loss $-\xi_j$ from raising the column variable.
Thus an optimum may be taken nonnegative.  Now truncate all variables
at $P$.  Feasibility again survives.  The zero-cost matching covers
every row that can have positive $\lambda_i$, and implies
\[
 \sum_i(\lambda_i-P)^+\le\sum_j(\xi_j-P)^+.
\]
The truncation therefore does not decrease the dual objective.
We may assume $0\le\lambda_i,\xi_j\le P$.

Put $S=\sum_{i\in A'}\lambda_i$.  The initial matching gives $D\le S$.
Disjointness of the comparator heads, and
$\xi_j\ge\lambda_i-\min(w_{ij},P)$, give
\[
 D\le-(r-2)S+\sum_{i\in B'}\min(\sigma_i,P)
       +P\sum_{i\in A'}(r-|H'_i|)^+
       +\sum_{i\in A'}\sum_{j\in H'_i}\min(w_{ij},P).
\]
Since $D\le S$ and $r\ge2$, multiplication yields
$(r-1)D$ at most the bracket in \eqref{simp:mandatory-bound}.
Duality proves the claim.  The initial owners are essential here:
arbitrary mandatory columns could not justify the negative-dual step.

\subsection{The algorithm and its proof}

\paragraph{Sample one ordinary matching.}
Forget the second capacity unit of every new mass.  The masses
$a_e+z_e$, together with the private masses $d_i$ on distinct private
dummy columns, form an ordinary fractional matching with every real
row covered.  Sample an integral matching $M$ with these label marginals.
This requires no explicit decomposition: pad to a square doubly
stochastic matrix, and repeatedly choose a fractional support cycle.
Move to its two maximal alternating endpoints, selecting them with
barycentric probabilities.  Each edge is a martingale, at least one
fractional edge disappears, and all row and column sums are preserved.
Parallel labels are allowed as two-edge cycles, or sampled conditional
on their combined edge.  There are polynomially many moves.  Discard
the padding rows afterward.  In particular every $A$ row and every
$B$ row with $\sigma_i>0$ is physically matched.

Every column with $\pi_j>K$ has no supported new mass by
\eqref{simp:new-price}.  Complementary slackness makes its ordinary
load one, so every sampled matching covers it by an ordinary label.
Lock those sampled edges: their rows use that one tight ordinary
program.  This can be imposed as fixed arcs of the single network
below.  For notation, remove their rows and columns, leaving $A',B'$
and the initial matching $M_0$.

\paragraph{Solve one flow.}
Make every physical column of $M_0$ mandatory.  Each $A'$ row has
its one sampled base label at cost zero, all candidates with
$c_{ij}\le T$ at cost $\theta c_{ij}$, and a capacity-one dummy of
cost $P$.  It has supply two.  Each $B'$ row has its supported
ordinary labels at cost zero and a private arc of cost $\sigma_i$;
it has supply one.  All physical columns have capacity one.
The initial matching plus one dummy on each $A'$ row is feasible.
Find an integral minimum-cost flow, of value $C(M)$.

A degree-two $A'$ row uses half of each of its selected labels.
A degree-one $A'$ row uses its base ordinary program, if selected,
or the specified ordinary fallback of its selected new label.
A $B'$ row uses its selected ordinary program or its private program.
Two half-new labels use one unit of capacity in each column, a
half-ordinary label uses half a unit, and a single ordinary label
uses one.  Thus the resulting cost-two labelled flow is feasible.
Only the sampled base edge can be ordinary on an unlocked $A'$ row,
so at most one ordinary minorant is ever combined with new minorants.
Lemma~\ref{simp:forest} and \eqref{simp:pointwise-contract} realize
this selected flow simultaneously.

\paragraph{Account for its score loss.}
Relative to row value $v_i$ and full capture of used item prices,
a half-new candidate loses at most $c_{ij}/2\le\theta c_{ij}$.
A single new-to-ordinary fallback loses at most
$L+c_{ij}-\pi_j\le P+\theta c_{ij}$; the last inequality follows
from $c_{ij}\le T$ and \eqref{simp:graph-parameters}.
A supported base new label has $c=0$.
A half-ordinary base label loses $\pi_j/2$.
An unmatched $B'$ row loses exactly $\sigma_i$.
Locked edges capture their row values and high prices in full.
No initially covered column is lost.  Consequently the repaired
logarithmic score $S(M)$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{simp:conditional-loss}
 J-S(M)\le C(M)+
   \sum_{j\notin\mathrm{im}(M)}\pi_j
   +\frac12\sum_{\substack{e\in M:\ i(e)\in A'\\e\text{ ordinary}}}\pi_{j(e)}.
\end{equation}

Apply \eqref{simp:mandatory-bound} to the heads in each $H_i$ that
remain in the unlocked graph and have $c_{ij}\le T$.
The initial count deficit pays $(P/(r-1))(r-h_i)^+$.
Every additional deleted head costs at most $P/(r-1)$.
A high-price deleted head is paid by $K/2$, once only by disjointness.
A retained low-price head is paid by $\kappa(1-e^{-c_{ij}})$:
the function $c/(1-e^{-c})$ is increasing, so
\eqref{simp:graph-parameters} gives
$\theta c/(r-1)\le\kappa(1-e^{-c})$ on $[0,T]$.
A deleted low-price head has $c>T$ and costs at most
$\kappa(1-e^{-T})$.  The candidate contract rules out any other
missing small-deficit head.  The private dummy costs give the second
term of \eqref{simp:repair-ledger}.

It remains to pay the last two terms of \eqref{simp:conditional-loss}.
Let $Z_j$ be the original new mass at column $j$, and $A_j^A$ the
original ordinary mass from rows of $A$.  At a positive-price column,
the original cost-two load is one.  Its ordinary load is $1-Z_j$,
so its probability of being unused by $M$ is $Z_j$.
All high-price columns are locked.  Hence the expected price loss is
\[
 \sum_{j:\pi_j\le K}\pi_j\left(Z_j+\frac12 A_j^A\right)
       \le\frac12\sum_{j:\pi_j\le K}\pi_j,
\]
because $2Z_j+A_j^A\le1$.  Combine this with the high-price charges,
the network bound, and the common realization to prove
Theorem~\ref{simp:repair-theorem}.

\paragraph{The NSW constants satisfy one short contract.}
Take
\[
 r=4,\quad K=\frac76L,\quad T=\frac{12}{5}L,\quad
 \theta=\frac7{10},\quad P=\frac{43}{25}L,\quad
 \kappa=\frac6{25},\qquad L=\log(1.00032357).
\]
Here $0<L<1/3000$, $P=L+(1-\theta)T$, and $P>\theta T$.
The two nontrivial inequalities reduce to
\[
 \frac{43}{75}<\frac7{12},\qquad
 \frac{43}{75}
 <\frac6{25}\frac{12}{5}
                  \left(1-\frac6{5\cdot3000}\right).
\]
They are rational comparisons; $1-e^{-T}\ge T-T^2/2$ proves the
exponential comparison.  No table of graph constants is needed.

\subsection{Finite weights and why one sample preserves the guarantee}

The theorem also applies to rational upper log weights.  Solve their
LP exactly and preserve every supported label; a support-size or
dual-stability assumption is unnecessary.  If every selected score
overstates its certified payoff by at most $\varepsilon_s$, then the
realization loses at most $n\varepsilon_s$, since each row's masses
sum to one.  For coherent new weights $n=\ell^{(.4)}+\Lambda$, their
supported prices obey $\pi_j\le\Lambda$ exactly.

Use rational repair constants $\Lambda<K_0$, $T_0\le T$, $P_0\le P$
with
\[
 P_0\ge\Lambda+(1-\theta)T_0,\qquad
 \frac{P_0}{r-1}\le\frac{K_0}{2},\qquad
 \frac{\max(P_0,\theta T_0)}{r-1}
                         \le\kappa(1-e^{-T_0}).
\]
Let computed deficits and private gaps obey $\widehat c\le c$ and
$\widehat\sigma\le\sigma$.  Assume every $\widehat c\le T_0$ candidate
has its local certificate.  The same proof pays the computed repair
by the original true ledger: deletion of a column locked above $K_0$
costs at most $P_0/(r-1)\le K_0/2\le\min(\pi_j,K)/2$;
retained deficits are smaller; and
a deleted low head has $c\ge\widehat c>T_0$ and hence pays its dummy
charge before the original arithmetic cap is reached.  The private
charges only decrease.  This is one-sided domination, not continuity
of a threshold indicator.

Condition on the entire online history and the selected residual
iterate $x$.  The matching sample has the prescribed ordinary label
marginals, and the minimum-cost repair for that sample is independent
of every comparator.  Thus \eqref{simp:repair-ledger} holds after
conditioning on $x$ and then averaging the sample.  Taking total
expectations over the uniformly selected iterate preserves the same
radial/regret bound as a deterministic best-of-all-components choice.
There is no need to evaluate any repaired component's true score.

An exact rational matching sampler can be replaced by its finite
dyadic implementation.  Its support still consists of feasible
matchings, and the total variation error is paid against the known
reservation/full-set logarithmic range.  Subsequent conditional
rounding is also feasible on every history.  Therefore the finite
expected-log guarantee and its Jensen consequence are unchanged up
to an arbitrary chosen error.  Finally, if one complete run has
$\mathbb E[\log\mathrm{OPT}-\log\mathrm{NSW}]\le\log R_\star+\xi$,
Markov amplification applies to that run, including its matching
sample.  Explicitly, for $0<\varepsilon\le1$, take
$\xi\le\varepsilon/12$ and $R_\star<2$.  If
$Y=\log(\mathrm{OPT}/\mathrm{NSW})\ge0$ and
$a=\log(R_\star+\varepsilon)$, then
\[
 a-\mathbb EY\ge
 \log(1+\varepsilon/R_\star)-\varepsilon/12
 \ge\varepsilon/4,\qquad a<3/2.
\]
Here $\log(1+x)\ge x/(1+x)$ gives the second inequality.
Thus $\Pr\{Y<a\}\ge1-\mathbb EY/a\ge\varepsilon/6$.
Taking $\lceil(6/\varepsilon)\log(1/\delta)\rceil$ independent
complete runs and retaining the actual largest utility product gives
success probability at least $1-\delta$.  No internal repaired-score
comparison is used.

The minimal algorithmic primitives of this section are: one hybrid
LP solve; ordinary bipartite matching sampling by cycle rounding;
one integral min-cost flow with lower bounds; the common one-function
forest program; and one final maximum-product reserved matching.
The first primitive is already used by the online update.  Only the
last four are executed at its one selected iterate.
