\begin{abstract}
We give a self-contained simplified proof of the tight infimum approximation
factor $R_\star=(4/(1-e^{-1})^3)^{1/4}=1.9948721225606033\ldots$ for the
matching--allocation--rematching framework for unweighted monotone
submodular Nash social welfare. The algorithm uses three ordinary physical
programs, one private program, a home option, and cost-two NEW labels.
One controller serves every row. Only after a residual iterate is selected
do we test one rectangle, sample one matching, and solve one minimum-cost
flow. A single pointwise completion theorem and two additive log minorants
supply a common feasible rounding. The controller has one compact endpoint
proof; one analytic band treats one through four comparator heads without
external positive-count prerequisites. A single directed verifier closes
the remaining bounded cases in 4,912,892 boxes. All finite score, profile,
constant and tail obligations are reproducible from a small standalone
certificate package. We retain polynomial-work rational value-oracle
implementations for every $R_\star+\varepsilon$, expected and amplified
high-probability guarantees, and prove the matching rational-answer
framework lower bound directly. Tightness is an exact infimum statement;
it does not assert a finite zero-tolerance endpoint.
\end{abstract}

\section{Result and algorithm}
For a feasible allocation $Z$, let
$\NSW(Z)=(\prod_{i=1}^nf_i(Z_i))^{1/n}$ and let $\OPT$ be its maximum.
Approximation factors are at least one. Put
\[
 \beta=1-e^{-1},\quad k=(4\beta)^{-1},\quad
 U_\star=\tfrac14(\log4-3\log\beta),\quad
 \lambda_\star=\tfrac14\log(4\beta),\quad R_\star=e^{U_\star}.
\]
Thus $U_\star-\lambda_\star=-\log\beta$ and
$U_\star+3\lambda_\star=\log4$ exactly. Throughout, finite decimal
algorithm parameters mean exact rationals, and $R_4=1.00032357$.

\begin{theorem}[Simplified tight approximation]\label{simp:main-theorem}
For normalized nonnegative monotone submodular valuations with rational
value-oracle answers, and every rational $\varepsilon>0$, the algorithm
below has an every-history feasible implementation with
\[
 \mathbb E\NSW(Z)\ge\OPT/(R_\star+\varepsilon).
\]
For every rational $0<\delta<1$, independent repetition and selection of
the largest actual welfare also achieve this factor with probability at
least $1-\delta$. Work is polynomial in the numbers of agents and items,
$1/\varepsilon$, $\log(1/\delta)$, and the bit lengths of the rational
inputs and oracle answers encountered. If $|M|\ge n$, the output gives
exactly one initially reserved item to each agent. The infimum achievable
factor in this framework is exactly $R_\star$, under either convention.
\end{theorem}

Here is the entire algorithm, with precise primitives proved below.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Reserve a maximum-product singleton matching and its protection
scales. Use the residual assignment polytope
$\mathcal P=\{x\ge0:\sum_i x_{ig}\le1\ (g\in M')\}$.
\item At each residual iterate, evaluate the private score, the three
ordinary physical programs, singleton and clipped HOME options, and eligible
NEW labels. Solve one cost-two auxiliary LP and retain one complementary
dual $(\log u,\pi)$.
\item On every row use the same controller
$\mu=\max\{k/\max(1,\alpha/u),r_b\}$, where the HOME root has target
$b=\min(\log(u/T_0),(43/25)\log R_4)/3$.
Choose the largest observable raw/contraction bonus, form its radial reward,
and make a projected residual update.
\item Select one iterate uniformly. On this iterate only, test
$A=\{F/u\le3/25,\ \alpha/u\le(21/20)R_4^2\}$.
Sample one ordinary matching with the auxiliary label marginals, and solve
one integral minimum-cost flow with its prescribed columns mandatory.
\item Round the resulting one-function row programs jointly on a forest.
Compute a maximum-product bijection of the reserved items to the disjoint
residual bundles, and assign any leftover residual items arbitrarily.
\end{enumerate}
The finite version uses one-sided enclosing tests and the explicit error
budget of Sections~\ref{simp:algorithm-interface} and \ref{simp:finite}.
The comparator allocation never enters the algorithm. The graph repair is
performed once, and no internal repaired-score comparison is required.

The proof has one chain: score primitives and compatible minorants feed the
one-flow repair; scalar row inequalities pay its single loss ledger;
radial regret and exact cancellation give the approximation. Zero heads
and the critical four-head face are analytic at the exact threshold.
Other cases use strict analytic reserves or complete directed covers.
The numerical certificates are mathematical reductions plus reproducible
interval computation, not a formal proof-assistant development.
